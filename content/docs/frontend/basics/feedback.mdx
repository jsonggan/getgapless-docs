---
title: Feedback and states
description: Why every action needs a visible response, and the states every screen must be designed for.
---

The most common reason an interface feels broken is silence. The user clicks, nothing visibly changes, and they click again or give up.

## Every action gets a response

People judge speed by what they see, not by what the system is doing. These response times are a long-standing rule of thumb:

| Wait | How it feels | What to show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 0.1 seconds | Instant | Nothing extra. The result is the feedback |
| Up to 1 second | A short pause, but the user stays focused | A subtle change, such as a pressed button or a small spinner |
| Up to 10 seconds | The user starts to wonder | Clear progress, and a way to cancel |
| Over 10 seconds | The user's attention drifts | Progress with detail, and let them do something else meanwhile |

AI responses often take several seconds. That is why most AI products show the answer word by word as it is written, instead of making the user stare at a spinner. See [Streaming](/docs/frontend/streaming).

## Every screen has more than one state

Designs often show only the ideal case: the screen full of perfect data. Real screens spend much of their time in other states, and each one needs a design.

| State | When it happens | What to show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty | First visit, or nothing has been created yet | What belongs here and how to start, such as a few example questions to ask |
| Loading | Waiting for data or a response | A placeholder in the shape of the content, or visible progress. Never a frozen screen |
| Partial | Some content has arrived, some has not | What you already have, with the rest clearly still coming |
| Error | Something failed | What happened in plain words, and what to do next |
| Success | The action worked | A quiet confirmation, such as a short message that fades |

An empty screen is a first impression. It is often the first thing a new user sees, so it deserves as much care as the full one. For the failures specific to AI products, see [Error and empty states](/docs/frontend/error-states).

## Writing error messages

- **Say what happened, in the user's words.** "We could not reach the server" instead of "Error 503".
- **Do not blame the user.** "That file type is not supported" instead of "You uploaded an invalid file".
- **Say what to do next.** Try again, wait a minute, or contact support.
- **Keep the user's work.** If a message fails to send, the text they typed must still be there.

## Perceived speed

How fast something feels matters more than how fast it is. Showing something useful right away makes the wait feel shorter:

- Show the user's own action at once, such as their message appearing in the chat before the server replies.
- Show placeholders in the shape of the coming content instead of a blank page.
- Show the first part of a result as soon as it exists.

See [Performance](/docs/frontend/performance) for how this applies to AI responses.
