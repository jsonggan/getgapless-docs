---
title: Usability
description: The habits that make a product feel obvious, so users succeed without instructions.
---

A usable product does not need a manual. Users reach their goal on the first try without thinking about the interface.

## Use patterns people already know

Users spend most of their time in other products, and they expect yours to work the same way. Search sits at the top. Settings hide behind a gear icon. The send button sits to the right of the text box, and Enter sends the message.

Every new pattern is something users must learn. Save your originality for what makes your product different, not for where the buttons go.

## Make clickable things look clickable

Buttons should look like buttons and links should look like links. Plain text that turns out to be clickable is invisible to most users, and decorative elements that look clickable but are not cause frustration.

## Fewer choices, clearer path

Every extra option slows the user down, because they must consider it before acting.

- Make one action the obvious next step, and keep the others visually secondary.
- Hide advanced options until someone asks for them. Most users of an AI assistant never need a setting for model temperature on the main screen.
- Remove options nobody uses. Each one costs attention from everyone who sees it.

## Clear words over clever words

- **Labels should say what happens.** "Delete chat" instead of "OK". "Upload document" instead of "Submit".
- **Use the user's words, not your team's.** Users upload "documents", they do not manage "embeddings" or "chunks".
- **Keep it short.** If a button needs a sentence to explain it, the button is the problem.

## Let people undo

People make mistakes. Undo is kinder than "Are you sure?" dialogs, which most users click through without reading.

Confirm only actions that cannot be undone, and say exactly what will be lost: "Delete this chat and its 24 messages? This cannot be undone."

## Do not make people remember

Show information instead of making users hold it in their heads. Keep the current context visible: which conversation they are in, which documents the answer can draw on, and which filters are on.

## Test with real people

You cannot judge your own product as a new user would, because you already know how it works. Give five people one task, watch them try it, and do not help. Every place they hesitate or guess wrong is a problem to fix.
