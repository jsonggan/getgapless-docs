## Hierarchy

The most important thing on the screen should be the most noticeable. You control this with size, weight, color, and position. In a chat app, the answer is the content, so controls stay small and muted.

<HierarchyDemo />

## Spacing

Padding and margin are the two tools for placing space.

<SpacingDemo />

Space groups things. Items placed close together read as related, and items placed apart read as separate.

<ProximityDemo />

Line items up along shared edges, and reuse a small set of spacing sizes everywhere.

<AlignmentDemo />

## Typography

Use one or two typefaces and three or four text sizes. Use weight and size for headings, not capitals or underlines.

<TypeScaleDemo />

Keep long text around 60 to 80 characters per line. AI answers are often long, so reading comfort matters more than usual.

<LineLengthDemo />

## Color and contrast

Use color for meaning, not decoration. It is why "Delete" and "Save" should never look the same.

<ColorMeaningDemo />

Keep text readable against its background. The common accessibility standard asks for a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text.

<ContrastDemo />

Never rely on color alone. About 1 in 12 men has some form of color blindness, so pair color with an icon or a word.

<ColorAloneDemo />

If you support light and dark mode, check both. A color that works on white can disappear on black.

<ThemeDemo />

## Consistency

The same thing should look and behave the same everywhere, so users learn your product once. Most teams get this from a design system or component library.
